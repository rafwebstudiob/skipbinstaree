Rubbish Removal Taree: Skip Bins vs Pick-up

In short: for a big clean-up, a skip bin is often the easier way to remove household or green waste in Taree. For small amounts, MidCoast Council's Taree Waste Management Centre and its yearly bulky waste collection are worth knowing about.

The main ways to get rid of household and garden rubbish in Taree, what each one takes, and when a skip bin makes more sense.

Last updated: 27 October 2026

Your options for rubbish removal in Taree

In short: For a big clean-up, a skip bin is usually the simplest way to remove household or garden waste. For a load or two, the Taree Waste Management Centre may be enough, and council's yearly bulky waste collection can take some furniture and whitegoods.

Most Taree households have three ways to get rid of more rubbish than fits in their bins:

  1. A skip bin, delivered to your driveway and taken away when you're done.
  2. Taking it to the tip yourself, at MidCoast Council's Taree Waste Management Centre at Tinonee.
  3. MidCoast's bulky waste collection, one free booked pick-up a year for eligible households.

Which one makes sense depends on how much you have, what it is, and whether you have a trailer and the time to do the runs.

Option 1: a skip bin

In short: A skip suits jobs with a lot of mixed rubbish, heavy waste or many trailer loads of garden waste, because you load it at your own pace and it's taken away in one go.

A skip sits on your driveway (or on the street with council approval) for an agreed hire period. You can fill it over a few days, and you don't need a trailer, a ute or anyone to lift heavy items. Skips can take things the bulky waste collection won't, such as bagged household rubbish, garden waste, soil, bricks and renovation waste, as long as you choose the right bin and tell the operator. They can't take asbestos, gas bottles, batteries, tyres or chemicals in a normal load. See what can go in a skip and the size guide.

For a deceased estate, a house move or a big shed or garage job, see our clean-outs guide.

Option 2: the Taree Waste Management Centre

In short: If you have a trailer and only a load or two, you can take rubbish to council's Taree Waste Management Centre yourself. Sorting your load can lower what you pay.

MidCoast Council's Taree Waste Management Centre is at 6843 The Bucketts Way, Tinonee. Council's 2025–26 brochure lists it as open 7am to 5pm every day. All of council's waste centres are closed on Christmas Day and Good Friday. It has a weighbridge, so you pay by the weight of your load, and a MidCoast Reviva reuse shop. It is one of only two council sites (with Gloucester) that accept asbestos, and asbestos disposal must be booked at least 24 hours ahead.

Council's "sort and save" approach means recyclables you separate before you arrive are charged less or nothing. Council lists free drop-off for domestic customers of household recycling (the same items as your yellow bin), clean scrap metal, household batteries, some e-waste, and good-condition items the gate staff accept for the reuse shop. Unsorted loads are charged at the general waste rate.

Option 3: MidCoast's free bulky waste collection

In short: Eligible households get one free booked bulky waste collection each financial year. It takes furniture, whitegoods and scrap metal, but not household rubbish, garden waste or building materials, and the pile has a size limit.

MidCoast Council lets eligible households book one free bulky waste collection per financial year at a time that suits them, and extra collections can be booked at a cost. Council's contractor confirms the collection week after you book. You put items out on the Sunday before that week, on the nature strip where your wheelie bins go, sorted into two piles: metal and whitegoods, and general and electronic waste.

  • Size limits: the total pile can't be bigger than a standard 7' x 4' trailer (about 2.1 m x 1.2 m), and each item must be under 30 kg. Smaller items like clothing and general household rubbish aren't collected.
  • It takes: furniture, lounges, tables, foam mattresses with no springs, floor coverings, small appliances, e-waste such as TVs and computers, and scrap metal and whitegoods (with doors and latches removed).
  • It won't take: wire-spring mattresses and bed bases, building and demolition materials, concrete, bricks, panes of glass, tyres and car parts, commercial waste, and hazardous waste such as paint, chemicals and asbestos. Council says one unacceptable item may mean the whole pile is rejected.

When you book, council says you can also drop off up to two mattresses (or one mattress and one bed base) free at your nearest Waste Management Centre.

What about my kerbside bins?

In short: Your bins are for everyday rubbish, recycling and garden clippings, not clean-ups.

MidCoast Council's standard service is a red-lid landfill bin collected weekly and a yellow-lid recycling bin collected fortnightly. Homes in the green waste collection zone also have a green-lid garden organics bin, collected fortnightly, for grass clippings, prunings, branches, leaves and weeds. Rural households have red and yellow bins only. Council says a green bin can't weigh more than 75 kg.

So which is easier?

In short: Pick a skip for big, mixed or heavy jobs. Use the tip or council's bulky collection for small amounts of the waste they accept.

  • A skip bin usually makes sense for a full clean-out, a renovation, a big garden or shed job, heavy waste, or when you don't have a trailer or the time for several tip runs.
  • The Taree Waste Management Centre suits one or two trailer loads, especially if you sort out the free recyclables first.
  • The bulky waste collection suits furniture, whitegoods and scrap metal that fit in one trailer-sized pile.

Frequently asked questions

What is the easiest way to get rid of a lot of rubbish in Taree?

It depends on how much you have and what it is. For one or two loads, a trailer run to the Taree Waste Management Centre at Tinonee, with your recyclables sorted out first, may be enough. For a big clean-out, heavy waste or many loads, a skip bin is often the easier option, because you load it at your own pace and it's taken away in one trip.

Where is the Taree tip and when is it open?

MidCoast Council's Taree Waste Management Centre is at 6843 The Bucketts Way, Tinonee. Council's 2025–26 brochure lists it as open 7am to 5pm every day. All council waste centres are closed on Christmas Day and Good Friday.

Does MidCoast Council pick up bulky rubbish?

Yes. Eligible households can book one free bulky waste collection per financial year, and extra collections can be booked at a cost. The pile must fit in a 7' x 4' trailer (about 2.1 m x 1.2 m), items must be under 30 kg, and household rubbish, garden waste, building materials, wire-spring mattresses and hazardous waste aren't accepted.

Can I put garden waste out with the bulky waste collection?

No. MidCoast's bulky waste collection is for household items, whitegoods and scrap metal. For garden waste, use your green bin if you have one, take it to a waste centre, or hire a green waste skip.

Can I take asbestos to the Taree tip?

MidCoast Council says asbestos is only accepted at its Taree and Gloucester Waste Management Centres, and disposal must be booked at least 24 hours ahead. Asbestos can never go in a general skip bin.

Can a skip bin take garden waste and building rubbish?

Usually, yes, which is one reason people choose a skip. Heavy waste such as soil, concrete and bricks normally needs a smaller bin with a weight limit, and asbestos can never go in a general skip. Tell the operator what you have when you request a quote.

Do I need a permit for a skip bin on the street in Taree?

A skip wholly on your own property generally doesn't need council approval. A skip on the road, footpath or verge usually needs approval from MidCoast Council. See our council skip permits guide for details.
