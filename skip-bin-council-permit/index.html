Skip bin permits in Taree: MidCoast Council rules

In short: What MidCoast Council requires, how to apply, and where to place a skip on your own property.

A skip on your own driveway usually doesn't need council approval. A skip on the road, footpath or nature strip is different. Here's what MidCoast Council says and what to check before you book.

Last updated: 24 October 2026

The short answer

On your own land, generally no. On the road, footpath or nature strip, MidCoast Council lists placing a waste storage container in a public place as an activity needing section 68 approval, and says you need its permission to temporarily occupy a road or footpath. Check with council before you book.

On your own property

The easiest spot is your own land: the driveway, front yard or a firm patch of lawn. Council's section 68 approval is for waste containers in a public place, so a skip that sits entirely on your property generally doesn't need it. Strata by-laws or development consent conditions can still apply. On low-lying blocks near the river, avoid soft ground after rain, and use planks to protect a concrete driveway.

On the road, footpath or nature strip

MidCoast Council lists "placing a waste storage container in a public place" as an activity that needs approval under section 68 of the Local Government Act 1993, lodged through the NSW Planning Portal. Council's road, footpath and drainage approvals page also says you need its permission for works or activities on a public road, footpath or nature strip, including temporarily occupying it.

Before you book a skip on the street, check with MidCoast Council about whether you need approval, the cost and how long it takes. Call (02) 7955 7777 (Monday to Friday, 8:30am to 4:30pm) or visit the MidCoast Council website. Mention it on the quote form too, because the operator may be able to advise on placement.

What to ask council before you book

  1. Does my spot need approval, and which one (section 68 of the Local Government Act, and whether a Roads Act section 138 consent also applies)?
  2. What does it cost this financial year, and how long does assessment take?
  3. How long can the skip stay, and are there conditions such as lights, reflectors or a set distance from the kerb?
  4. Are there extra rules for a busy road, a bus stop or a heritage area?

Ask for the answers in writing or note the date and who you spoke to.

Placing the skip safely

Keep the skip clear of driveways, hydrants, bus stops and drains. Don't fill it above the rim. On river flats, choose a high, firm spot that won't turn to mud, and put planks under the bin on concrete.
